Humming but not grinding

A disposal that hums and does not spin is jammed. Cut the power at the wall switch and the breaker. Then look underneath for a hex socket in the center of the motor housing. A hex wrench turned back and forth frees most jams. Press the red reset button on the base afterward.

Never reach into the grinder, even if the power is off. Use tongs or pliers to remove an object you can see. If the unit hums again, or the reset trips repeatedly, the motor or the bearings may be failing, and replacement may be the answer.

Leaks: where the water is coming from

Where it leaksLikely causeTypical fix
Top, at the sink flangeDried plumber's putty or loose mountingReseat the flange.
Side, at the dishwasher hoseLoose clamp or cracked inletTighten or replace the hose.
Bottom of the unitFailed seal, shell crackedReplace the disposal.
Drain pipe connectionLoose gasket or nutReseal the joint.

Water from the bottom of the body means the internal seal has failed. That cannot be repaired. The only fix is a new unit, and delay risks cabinet damage and mold.

Slow drains and clogs in the sink

If the sink drains slowly when the disposal runs, the trap or the line beyond it may be partly blocked. Grease and starchy waste such as rice and potato peels are the usual causes. Fibrous foods such as celery and corn husks wrap around the shredder.

A disposal does not clear a clogged drain. If the water backs up into the other side of a double sink, the line needs drain cleaning, not a new disposal.

Choosing a replacement unit

Horsepower, feed type and noise insulation separate the models. A continuous-feed unit with a 1/2 horsepower motor suits light use. A larger motor handles heavier loads and runs quieter. Batch-feed units run only when a stopper is in place, which some households prefer for safety.

Installation includes a new mounting kit, a new drain connection, an electrical connection and a dishwasher knockout opening if a dishwasher is connected. The old unit is hauled away.

What never belongs in a disposal

  • Cooking grease and oil.
  • Coffee grounds in quantity.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels.
  • Bones, fruit pits and shells.
  • Fibrous vegetables such as celery stalks.
  • Glass, plastic or metal.

A disposal protects the sink from small food scraps. It does not turn the kitchen drain into a trash chute, and the line beyond it pays the price. Many Carol Stream kitchens were built decades ago with narrower drain lines than newer ones, so be gentle.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Switch off the disposal at the wall switch and the breaker.
  • Never put a hand into the chamber.
  • Stop running the dishwasher if the sink backs up.
  • Move cleaning supplies out of the cabinet.
  • Wipe up water to prevent slips and cabinet damage.

These steps take two minutes and protect you and the cabinet while help is on the way.

Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation questions

Do you handle garbage disposal emergencies at night or on weekends in Carol Stream?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and the dispatcher arranges a plumber, same day when available.

Can a jammed disposal be fixed without replacing it?

Often yes. Most jams clear with a hex wrench and a reset. A leaking body cannot be repaired.

Is it safe to run a disposal that leaks?

No. Turn it off and shut off the water to the sink until it is repaired.

How long does a disposal last?

Many last about eight to fifteen years, depending on use and what goes in.

Do I need an electrician to replace it?

A plug-in swap is usually a plumber's job. If the circuit needs work, the plumber will tell you.
