Where Fair Oaks is in Carol Stream

The Carol Stream Park District lists three parks that carry the name. Fair Oaks Central is at 1320 Birchbark Trail, Fair Oaks East is at 1284 Birchbark Trail and Fair Oaks West is at 700 Fair Oaks Road. Evergreen Lakes Park and Stonebridge Park sit on the same Birchbark Trail.

Census geocoding places 1320 Birchbark Trail inside the village limits, on the western side of Carol Stream. No public source we found draws a boundary around Fair Oaks, so this page describes it by those anchors and does not claim exact edges.

Two housing eras in one name

A real estate data site dates the Fair Oaks subdivision to roughly 1980 to 1982. That is a listing-data figure, so treat it as approximate. Homes of that age are now more than four decades old, and a house that old has usually had several fixtures, valves and water heaters replaced already.

The newer part is the Villas of Fair Oaks. The Daily Herald reported that the village board approved the subdivision on June 3, 2024. It sits at the northeast corner of Lies Road and Fair Oaks Road on 9.19 acres, with 32 duplex units in 16 buildings built by Pulte Homes and a 1.93 acre retention pond. Listing sites show the first sales in 2024 and 2025.

A few steps limit damage in any home. None of them need tools.

Water and the first things to check

The Village of Carol Stream buys Lake Michigan water through the City of Chicago, and its 2025 water quality report says it has no lead service lines in its system. The DuPage Water Commission describes its lake water at about 8 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard.

The village report does not list a hardness number. A simple tap test tells you what your own water does to a heater. Public Works can answer questions at 630-871-6260, and a water heater repair visit usually starts with the tank age and any scale.

Living in a new townhome

Attached homes share walls and sometimes a roof line, so a leak can reach a neighbor quickly. Learn where your unit shutoff is before you need it, and keep the builder's paperwork for the water heater and fixtures.

  • Find the unit shutoff and test that it turns.
  • Photograph the water heater label with its model and date.
  • Ask the association which exterior spigots and lines it maintains.
  • Report a ceiling stain to the association and to the neighbor above or below.

Warranty terms differ by builder and by item, so we do not state them here. Ask your builder or association what is still covered before you pay for a repair.
