Why sump pumps matter here

Carol Stream sits in the Klein Creek and Thunderbird Creek area, and the April 18, 2013 flood put at least four village homes under water, according to the Daily Herald. That storm closed 25 roads in Carol Stream. DuPage County later finished the Armstrong Park reservoirs in 2015, which hold about 37 million gallons and protect nearly 60 structures in the Klein Creek watershed.

Public projects reduce risk but do not stop water from reaching a basement through the soil. A working sump pump is still the first line of defense inside the house.

Quick checks before you call

  1. Confirm the pump is plugged in and the outlet works, then reset the GFCI or breaker.
  2. Lift the float by hand. The pump should start.
  3. Listen for humming without pumping, which suggests a jammed impeller or a failed check valve.
  4. Look at the discharge pipe outside to make sure it is not frozen or blocked.
  5. If water keeps rising, call the 24/7 line and move belongings off the floor.

Never stand in standing water near electrical equipment. If the basin or outlet is under water, leave the pump alone and call.

Pumps wear out. Many last around ten years, so an older unit that struggled last spring is a good candidate to replace before the next storm.

Battery backup and water-powered backup

Heavy storms often cause power outages at the same time, and a pump with no power cannot run. A battery backup system keeps a second pump running for hours. A water-powered backup uses municipal water pressure and needs no electricity, though it requires adequate pressure and a proper discharge setup.

Ask which fits your basement. The plumber looks at the pit size, the discharge route and your electrical setup before recommending anything.

Pits, discharge and drain tiles

A pump is only as good as the pit and discharge around it. A shallow or cracked pit, a discharge line that runs too close to the foundation, or a clogged drain tile can send water straight back to the house. The discharge should end well away from the foundation and not toward a neighbor.

For a deeper moisture problem that a pump alone does not solve, see basement waterproofing. For a sewer line that backs up instead, see sewer line cleaning.

Sump Pump Repair and Installation questions

Do you handle sump pump emergencies at night or on weekends in Carol Stream?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes sump pump calls at any hour, and the dispatcher sends a licensed plumber, same day when available.

How long does a sump pump last?

Many last roughly ten years, depending on use. Pumps in busy pits wear out sooner.

Do I need a battery backup?

If your basement has flooded before or storms often cut power, a backup is worth discussing.

Why does my pump run when it is not raining?

A stuck float, a failed check valve or a high water table can cause it. A plumber can tell which.

Can you install a pump in a basement that has none?

Yes, if there is a drain tile or a place for a pit. The plumber assesses it on the visit.
