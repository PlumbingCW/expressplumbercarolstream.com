ABOUT · CAROL STREAM, IL

About Carol Stream Express Plumbing

Carol Stream Express Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing line based in Carol Stream, Illinois. We answer the phone, learn what is wrong and dispatch a licensed plumber.

Call (630) 822-929024/7 Emergency Line Open

How we work

Everything starts with a phone call. We have no web forms, because a person describing a leak or a backup gets help faster than a form that waits in an inbox.

The dispatcher takes your address, the symptom and any safety concerns, then sends a plumber. The plumber explains the cause and the price before starting work.

What happens on a call

  1. A dispatcher answers and asks what is happening and whether water is spreading.
  2. You are given quick safety steps, such as closing the main valve.
  3. A licensed plumber is assigned and the dispatcher gives you an honest arrival window.
  4. The plumber diagnoses the problem and explains the repair and what drives the price.
  5. You approve the work, the plumber completes and tests it, and explains what to watch afterward.

We do not promise arrival times we cannot keep. If the line is busy or the weather is bad, the dispatcher says so.

What we cover

We handle drains, water heaters, leaks, sewer lines, sump pumps, repiping, fixtures and more. See all services for the full list and service areas for the towns we reach.

We serve Carol Stream and nearby towns, including Wheaton, Glendale Heights, Bloomingdale, West Chicago and Hanover Park.

Where we are

Our address is 505 E North Ave, Carol Stream, IL 60188. North Avenue is the village's main commercial road. Find directions on the contact page.

Carol Stream was incorporated in 1959 and sits across Bloomingdale, Milton and Wayne townships. We know the area because we work in it.

The village we work in

Carol Stream began in the late 1950s, when Jay W. Stream of Durable Construction started buying farmland in 1957. The first residents, the Blum family, moved in on November 1, 1958, and the first village board meeting was held on February 12, 1959.

The population grew from 836 in 1960 to 31,716 in 1990 and was 39,854 in 2020. The village has parks such as Armstrong Park, Aldrin Community Center and Coral Cove Water Park. Homes from those growth decades are now the ones that need new water heaters and drain work.

Water in Carol Stream

The village buys Lake Michigan water through the City of Chicago and says in its 2025 water quality report that it has no lead service lines in its system. It also keeps a service line material inventory that Public Works can share. Public Works can be reached at 630-871-6260.

The DuPage Water Commission describes its lake water at about 8 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard. Carol Stream's report does not list hardness, so we suggest testing your own tap.

Licensing in Illinois

Illinois licenses plumbers through the Department of Public Health under 225 ILCS 320, and plumbing work follows the standards of the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890. You can verify a plumber's license on the department's online lookup before work begins.

Why a phone line and not a form

Plumbing emergencies change by the minute. A form sits until someone reads it, and it cannot ask the follow-up question that matters. A phone call can.

Is the water coming from a pipe or a fixture? Is it clear or brown? Can you reach the main valve? Those answers decide what the plumber brings and what you should do while you wait.

What we will not do

  • Pressure you into work you do not need.
  • Quote a price you cannot explain.
  • Promise an arrival time we cannot keep.
  • Ask you to fill out a form to get help.
  • Hide what the plumber found.

If you ever feel unsure about a recommendation, ask the plumber to show you the problem and explain the alternatives.
