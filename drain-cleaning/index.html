When a clogged drain is an emergency

A single slow bathroom sink can wait for a daytime appointment. A drain that sends water back up another fixture cannot. If the tub fills when the washing machine drains, or the basement floor drain bubbles when someone flushes, the blockage sits in the main line and every fixture upstream is feeding it.

Treat these signs as urgent: sewage smell with standing water, more than one fixture backing up at once, water rising in the lowest drain in the house, or a toilet that will not clear after plunging. Call the 24/7 line, then stop using water until the plumber arrives. Same-day service is the normal goal, and after-hours calls are answered by a dispatcher, not a voicemail box.

  • Stop running sinks, showers, dishwashers and washing machines.
  • Leave the toilet alone if water is near the rim.
  • Move rugs and stored boxes away from the lowest floor drain.
  • Note which fixtures are slow and which are fully blocked.

Kitchen, bathroom and laundry clogs

Kitchen lines clog with grease that cooled inside the pipe, bathroom lines with hair wrapped around a pop-up stopper or a trap, and laundry lines with lint and detergent residue. Each one is cleared differently, so the first question from the plumber is which fixture is slow and how long it has been that way.

A kitchen line that drains slowly after every dishwasher cycle usually has a grease collar a few feet down. A tub that drains in a swirl and then stalls often has hair at the strainer or at the first bend. A clog at a single fixture is a branch problem. Slow drains at every fixture point to the main line, and that is a different job.

What a cable, a snake and a jet each do

A hand auger clears hair and soft clogs close to the fixture. A motorized cable runs further into the branch line and cuts through roots, scale and compacted debris. A water jet scours the pipe wall clean, which is why it is the better choice for grease. See hydro jetting when a line keeps clogging every few weeks.

Main-line clogs and the signs that point to roots

The main line carries every drain in the house out to the lateral that meets the village sewer. Carol Stream was incorporated in 1959 and grew from 4,434 residents in 1970 to 31,716 in 1990, so a large share of the housing stock and its lateral pipe is now several decades old. Joints in older clay, cast iron or early plastic laterals are where tree roots tend to find their way in.

Roots show up as a pattern. The line clears, then clogs again within weeks or months. A cable brings back stringy fibers. The toilet gurgles when the shower drains. When that pattern appears, a sewer camera inspection shows what is actually in the pipe, and it keeps you from paying for cleaning that will not last.

What the plumber checks before cleaning

Where the cleanout is, usually near the foundation wall or in the basement floor.

Whether the blockage is in a branch or in the main line.

Whether recent heavy rain, a failing sump discharge or a collapsed section could be involved.

Keeping drains clear after the visit

Most repeat clogs have a habit behind them. Grease poured down the kitchen sink, wipes labeled flushable, and hair with no strainer are the three that plumbers see again and again. A drain screen in each tub and shower costs a few dollars and stops most of the hair.

If a line has been cleared twice in a year, schedule the camera before the third call. If a toilet is involved, toilet repair covers the flange and fill valve problems that mimic a clog. If water is also showing up on the basement floor, read about sump pump repair before you decide the drain is the only cause.

Drain Cleaning questions

Do you handle drain cleaning emergencies at night or on weekends in Carol Stream?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. Call (630) 822-9290, describe which fixtures are backing up, and the dispatcher sends a licensed plumber the same day when one is available.

How do I know if the clog is in the main line?

If more than one fixture is slow, or the floor drain bubbles when you run a sink, the main line is the likely cause. A single slow fixture usually means a branch clog.

Is a chemical drain cleaner a good idea first?

Usually not. Chemical cleaners can sit in a fully blocked pipe, burn skin on contact and make the plumber's work riskier. Plunging gently is the safer first step.

Will the plumber explain the price before starting?

Yes. You hear the price and the method before work begins, and any change to that plan is discussed first.

Can drain cleaning fix a line that keeps clogging?

It clears the blockage but cannot repair a broken or root-invaded pipe. If the clog returns, a camera inspection shows whether the pipe needs repair or lining.
