How to Use a Plumbing Snake on a Clogged Drain in Carol Stream, IL

A snake clears many clogs, but the wrong tool or technique can scratch a tub or push a blockage deeper. Here is how to pick the right one, how to use it and how to know when to stop.

Match the snake to the drain

ToolBest forAvoid on
Hand-crank drain snakeSinks, tubs and showersMain sewer lines.
Closet augerToiletsAnything but toilets.

A closet auger has a protective sleeve that keeps porcelain from scratching. Never use a regular snake in a toilet, because it can scar the bowl.

Before you start

Put on gloves and eye protection. Lay towels around the work area. Remove the pop-up stopper or strainer. For a sink, you may need to remove the P-trap and clear it first.

Skip chemical drain cleaners before snaking. The chemicals can splash back and burn skin, and they sit in the pipe and make the work dangerous.

Step by step on a sink or tub

  1. Feed the snake slowly into the drain until you feel resistance.
  2. Tighten the thumb screw about six inches from the opening.
  3. Turn the handle clockwise while pushing gently.
  4. When the cable breaks through or snags, pull it back slowly.
  5. Remove debris, run hot water and test the drain.

If the snake stops hard and will not turn, do not force it. A cable can kink and become stuck. Back out and call a plumber.

Signs you need more than a snake

  • The clog returns within days.
  • More than one fixture is slow.
  • A toilet gurgles when you run a sink.
  • The snake comes back with roots.
  • Water backs up from a floor drain.

Those signs point to a main line problem. A camera inspection shows the cause, and sewer line cleaning or hydro jetting may be needed.

Why chemicals and snakes do not mix

A snake can break a clog into pieces, but it leaves a thin channel through grease. Grease then rebuilds on the pipe wall. Hot water and enzyme cleaners do little on heavy buildup.

A hydro jet scours the entire wall of the pipe. For kitchen lines with repeat grease, drain cleaning with the right method gives longer relief.

Keeping drains clear afterward

Use hair catchers in showers, avoid pouring grease down the sink, run hot water after washing dishes and avoid flushing wipes labeled flushable. They do not break down like toilet paper.

If your home is in an older area like West Chicago, expect more root intrusion. A yearly check can catch it before a backup.

Using a closet auger on a toilet

  1. Flush once to see where the water goes, then stop if it rises.
  2. Pull the auger handle back so the cable retracts into the sleeve.
  3. Insert the curved tip into the drain opening.
  4. Crank the handle while pushing gently until the cable moves freely.
  5. Pull the cable out, flush and test.

A toilet clog that returns often points to a drain or vent problem. Do not keep augering. A camera shows what is happening.

Cleaning a tub or shower drain

Most tub clogs are hair wrapped around the stopper or in the overflow tube. Remove the stopper and clean it. Then feed a narrow snake or a plastic drain tool into the drain and pull the hair out.

Do not use a large power snake on a tub drain. The cable can scratch the finish or jam in the trap. If the tub still drains slowly, call for a camera and a proper cleaning.

What a pro machine can do that a hand snake cannot

A drum machine runs a longer, thicker cable with a variety of cutting heads. It can clear roots and scale in a main line. A camera feeds back a view of the pipe so the plumber can see what the cutter is doing.

A hydro jet uses water at high pressure to wash the whole pipe wall. Choosing the tool depends on what the camera shows. Ask which method is being used and why.

Protecting your pipes while you work

  • Never run the snake with the power on while holding the cable by hand.
  • Wipe the cable as you retract it to keep the mess down.
  • Use a bucket under the trap to catch water.
  • Rinse the cable and dry it before storing.
  • Replace a cable that is kinked or rusted.

A little care keeps both your pipes and your tools in good shape. If you are unsure, stop and call.

When to stop and call

  • The cable will not advance or turn.
  • You hear a crack or feel a sudden give.
  • Water comes back with sewage smell.
  • Several fixtures are affected.
  • You have tried twice and the clog returns.

Pushing harder can damage the pipe or the tool. A plumber has cameras and cutters made for the job.

Renting a machine

Hardware stores rent power augers for main line cleaning. They are heavy, they spin fast and they can injure a hand or damage a pipe. Rental shops often explain the basics, but experience matters.

If your clog is in the main line, a plumber with a camera is usually the safer and quicker choice. Renting only makes sense for confident do-it-yourselfers with a clear path to the cleanout.

Clogs in the next town over

Slow drains are just as common in Hanover Park, where older branch lines gather grease and hair.

The same rule applies there. If a snake clears the clog and it returns, ask for a camera look.

Trees, roots and Carol Stream laterals

Carol Stream grew from 836 residents in 1960 to 31,716 in 1990, so many of its laterals are decades old and the trees planted along its streets have had time to mature.

Roots seek moisture and enter pipe joints. If a snake keeps returning roots, that is a lateral problem and not a household clog. Ask the plumber to run a camera before another cleaning.

Questions readers ask

Do you handle clogged drain emergencies at night in Carol Stream?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes calls at any hour.

Will a snake scratch my tub?

A careful hand snake with a clean tip should not, but go slowly.

Can I use a snake on a toilet?

Use a closet auger. A regular snake can scratch the bowl.

How far can a hand snake reach?

Typically twenty-five feet or so. Longer runs need a machine.

What if the snake gets stuck?

Stop and call a plumber. Forcing it can damage the pipe.
