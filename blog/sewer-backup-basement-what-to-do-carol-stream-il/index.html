Sewer Backup in a Carol Stream, IL Basement: What to Do First

Sewage in a basement is a health hazard and a stressful emergency. The order of your first moves matters, and this guide gives them in the sequence that protects people first, property second.

Safety comes first

Sewage carries bacteria and viruses. Keep children and pets away. If water is near outlets or appliances, do not enter until power is off, and only switch off the breaker if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

If anyone feels ill after contact, contact a doctor. Wear rubber boots, gloves and a mask if you must enter.

Stop adding water

  1. Stop flushing toilets and running sinks, showers, dishwashers and washers.
  2. If water is entering from a floor drain, do not use any fixtures.
  3. Close the main water valve if you cannot stop the flow of used water.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher it is a sewage backup.
  5. Open windows for ventilation if the weather allows.

Every flush sends more water into the same blocked line. Stopping use is the fastest way to limit the volume.

If neighbors are affected, call the village Public Works as well. A plumber's camera inspection shows where the blockage is, and that determines who handles the repair.

Get the line cleared and inspected

A plumber clears the blockage with a cable machine or a hydro jet, then runs a camera to see why it formed. Roots, grease, broken pipe and bellies leave different pictures.

See sewer line cleaning and sewer inspection for what each service covers. If the camera shows damage, sewer line repair explains your options.

Cleanup and documentation

  • Photograph and video everything before cleanup.
  • Remove wet porous items such as carpet and cardboard.
  • Disinfect hard surfaces with a suitable cleaner.
  • Dry the space with fans and a dehumidifier.
  • Keep receipts for the plumber, cleaning and replacements.

Porous materials soaked in sewage usually cannot be saved. Professional restoration companies handle serious cases.

Insurance and prevention

Standard homeowner policies often exclude sewer backup unless a rider is added. Call your insurer early and ask what is covered. After the cleanup, ask the plumber about a backwater valve, which stops sewage from flowing back into the house.

In towns near Carol Stream like Hanover Park and West Chicago, the same steps apply. A backwater valve is a plumbing installation described under plumbing installation.

Why backups happen

  • Tree roots entering joints in the lateral.
  • Grease buildup in the main line.
  • A broken or collapsed pipe.
  • Wipes and other items flushed down toilets.
  • Heavy rain that overloads the sewer system.

The cause decides the repair. A cleaning clears the blockage, and a repair fixes the pipe. Without a camera, you are guessing.

How a backwater valve helps

A backwater valve has a flap that opens for outflow and closes if sewage tries to flow back. It sits in the lateral or in the basement floor drain line. It must be accessible for cleaning.

A valve does not stop a blockage in your own line, and it does not fix cracks. It is protection against a backup from the main. A plumber tells you whether it suits your house.

What to tell the dispatcher

  1. Your address and which fixtures are affected.
  2. Whether sewage is on the floor and how much.
  3. When it started and whether it follows rain.
  4. Whether neighbors report the same problem.
  5. Anything that was flushed or poured down the drain recently.

Clear information lets the dispatcher send the right equipment the first time.

Health precautions during cleanup

  • Wear rubber gloves, boots and eye protection.
  • Do not eat, drink or smoke in the work area.
  • Wash hands and any exposed skin with soap and clean water.
  • Wash clothing separately in hot water.
  • Keep children and pets out until cleaning is done.

Sewage contains pathogens that cause stomach illness and skin infections. If you cannot clean safely, call a restoration company.

Following up after the repair

After the line is cleared, ask the plumber for a written summary of the cause and what was done. If a camera found damage, schedule the repair soon. A cleaned but damaged pipe will back up again.

Check with your insurer about any claim deadlines. Save the invoices, photos and the camera video.

Calls to make after a backup

  1. Call the 24/7 plumbing line first.
  2. Call your insurer to report the loss.
  3. Call Public Works if neighbors are affected.
  4. Call a restoration company for serious damage.
  5. Call your mortgage servicer if the loss is large.

Keep a log of calls with dates and names. It helps if there is a dispute later.

Protecting the floor drain

Floor drains in basements are the first place a backup shows up. A plug-style check valve in the drain can stop sewage from rising, but it needs cleaning and does not replace a backwater valve in the line.

Do not seal a floor drain with a hard cap, because water from a leak or washer discharge then has nowhere to go. Ask a plumber what suits your layout.
