Water Softener and Tankless Water Heater in Carol Stream, IL: Do You Need Both?

Tankless water heaters promise endless hot water, but scale can shorten their life. Here is how hardness works, what Carol Stream's water reports say and how to decide whether a softener belongs in the plan.

Why scale matters more to a tankless heater

A tank heater holds a large volume of water and heats it slowly, so scale settles at the bottom. A tankless heater pushes water through a small heat exchanger at high temperature. Minerals drop out of the water right on the exchanger walls and narrow the passages.

As the passages narrow, flow drops and the unit works harder. Error codes, lukewarm water and eventually a failed exchanger can follow. Descaling slows the process, and softening the water can prevent much of it.

What hardness means in numbers

Hardness is the amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium. It is reported in grains per gallon or in milligrams per liter as calcium carbonate. A common rule of thumb calls under 3.5 grains soft, 3.5 to 7 moderately hard, 7 to 10.5 hard and above that very hard.

Your own tap can differ from the supplier's average because of home plumbing and any softener already installed. A test strip from a hardware store gives a rough reading, and a lab test gives a better one.

Manufacturer warranties sometimes require water below a certain hardness or an installed treatment system. Read the warranty before you buy. Tankless water heater work and water softener and filtration are often planned together.

The decision, step by step

  1. Test your tap or get lab results.
  2. Read the heater manufacturer's water requirements.
  3. Compare the cost of a softener with the cost of yearly descaling.
  4. Decide on whole-house softening or an inline scale filter.
  5. Schedule installation so the heater and softener share a bypass and drain plan.

If you are replacing a storage tank heater with a tankless one, plan the water treatment at the same time. It is easier to add valves and a drain while the pipes are already open.

Other things to plan for with a tankless heater

Tankless units need a gas line sized for the burner, a venting route and a condensate drain if they are condensing. Electric models need heavy circuits that many panels cannot supply. A plumber checks all of this before quoting.

A storage heater may be the better fit if your gas line or electrical service is limited. See water heater installation for what the visit covers.

Salt, brine and drains

A salt-based softener discharges brine to a drain during regeneration. It needs a drain connection with an air gap and a nearby outlet. Homes served by the village sewer can usually accept this discharge. Check local rules if you are unsure.

A bypass valve lets you isolate the softener for service or for outdoor watering. Ask your plumber to install one, because it makes future repairs much easier.

A real example of scale math

Suppose water at 8 grains per gallon passes through a heater that handles 40,000 gallons a year. That is 320,000 grains of hardness minerals moving through the unit every year. Not all of it deposits, but even a small share builds a layer.

That layer acts like insulation. The heater compensates by firing longer, which raises gas use and stresses the exchanger. Cutting the hardness by softening or by scale control reduces that burden.

Signs your tankless heater is scaling up

  • Water temperature swings or runs lukewarm.
  • Flow drops at the shower.
  • Error codes mentioning the heat exchanger or flow.
  • Banging or kettle-like noises.
  • Rising gas bills with no change in use.

Descaling with a pump and a mild acid solution restores many units. Your plumber can show you how often it is needed in your home.

Softener sizing basics

Softeners are sized by the number of people in the house, the hardness and the daily water use. An oversized unit wastes salt, and an undersized unit runs out of capacity and lets hardness through.

A plumber can set the regeneration schedule to match your use. Ask how many grains of capacity the unit has and how often it will regenerate.

What to expect during the install visit

  1. The plumber confirms the plan and shuts off the water.
  2. Bypass valves and a drain connection are installed.
  3. The softener is connected and the control is set for your hardness.
  4. The tankless heater is mounted, vented and connected.
  5. Both systems are tested and you are shown how they work.

Plan for the water to be off for several hours. Ask about the order of work if you also plan to remove an old tank.

Planning the order of work

If you are adding both, install the softener first so the water reaching the heater is already soft. Leave room for service valves on both. Ask the plumber to label the bypass and drain.

Schedule the visit when the household can be without hot water for a few hours.

Water supply differences by town

Glendale Heights buys water from the DuPage Water Commission, while West Chicago uses treated groundwater. That changes how hard the water is likely to be at your tap.

Wherever you live, test the tap before you size a softener or choose a tankless unit.

LOCAL NOTE · CAROL STREAM, IL

What the Carol Stream and DuPage reports say

Carol Stream's 2025 water quality report says the village buys Lake Michigan water through the City of Chicago and treats it with chlorine and fluoride. The report does not list a hardness figure.

The DuPage Water Commission says on its FAQ that its Lake Michigan water is about 8 grains per gallon, or 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate. That is a commission statement, not a Carol Stream figure, so test your own tap before sizing a softener.

Questions readers ask

Is Carol Stream water hard enough to need a softener?

The DuPage Water Commission describes its water at about 8 grains per gallon. Carol Stream's own report lists no hardness, so test your tap.

Can I install a tankless heater without a softener?

Yes, but plan for regular descaling and check the warranty terms.

Does a softener remove chlorine?

No. A carbon filter handles chlorine taste and smell.

How often should a tankless heater be flushed?

Manufacturers commonly suggest once a year in harder water. Follow the manual for your model.

Does Carol Stream have lead service lines?

The village's 2025 report says it has no lead service lines in its system.
