Water Softener and Filtration in Carol Stream, IL

Water softener and filtration work in Carol Stream starts with a question: what is actually in your water? The answer decides whether you need a softener, a filter, both or neither.

What the local water looks like

Carol Stream buys treated Lake Michigan water through the City of Chicago, according to the village's 2025 water quality report. The village treats it with chlorine and fluoride, with a reported fluoride maximum of 0.72 ppm. That report does not list hardness.

The DuPage Water Commission says on its FAQ that Lake Michigan water in its system is about 8 grains per gallon, or 137 mg/L as calcium carbonate. That is moderately hard. Your own tap can differ, so a test strip or a lab test is the honest starting point.

Whole-house or under-sink

A whole-house system treats every tap, shower and appliance. It protects the water heater and fixtures from scale. An under-sink filter treats only the drinking tap, which is simpler and cheaper.

If your main complaint is taste, an under-sink unit may be enough. If scale is building up on faucets, in the dishwasher and in the water heater, think about a softener. See tankless water heater for why scale matters on high-efficiency heaters.

What installation involves

  1. Test or review your water results.
  2. Choose the system and location, with a nearby drain and power outlet.
  3. Install bypass valves so you can isolate the unit.
  4. Connect the drain line with an air gap where required.
  5. Set the hardness and regeneration schedule and show you how to refill salt.

Do not drain softener brine into a septic system without checking local rules. Carol Stream homes are on the village sewer, but confirm for your address.

Maintenance and cost of ownership

A salt-based softener needs salt, and the brine discharge adds sodium to the drain. Filters need cartridges on schedule. Skipping changes lets the filter clog or stop working.

  • Check salt level monthly.
  • Replace carbon and sediment cartridges on the manufacturer's schedule.
  • Sanitize the system annually if recommended.
  • Keep the bypass valve accessible.

Choosing a salt or salt-free system

Salt-based softeners remove hardness minerals by ion exchange. Salt-free systems condition the water so scale clings less, but they do not remove hardness. Which suits you depends on whether you want soft water or only less scale buildup.

Ask the plumber to explain what the system does to the minerals and what you will maintain. Be wary of any promise that sounds too good to be true.

What to do if the softener or filter leaks

  • Put the unit in bypass or close its inlet valve.
  • Unplug the controller if water is near it.
  • Place a bucket under the leak.
  • Wipe up brine and water to avoid slips.
  • Call to schedule a visit.

A leaking unit is rarely an emergency, but water on the floor still causes damage.

Water Softener and Filtration questions

Is Carol Stream water hard?

The DuPage Water Commission describes its water at about 8 grains per gallon. Carol Stream's own report does not list hardness, so test your tap.

Do I need a softener?

Only if hardness causes problems you can see, such as scale. Test first.

Does a softener make water safe to drink?

A softener targets hardness, not contaminants. Use a filter if taste or a specific contaminant is the concern.

Can you install a filter I already bought?

Yes. Tell the dispatcher the model and bring the paperwork.

Does Carol Stream have lead service lines?

The village's 2025 report says it has no lead service lines in its system. Ask Public Works for the inventory for your address.
