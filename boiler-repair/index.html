Boiler Repair in Carol Stream, IL

Boiler repair in Carol Stream covers hot-water heating systems with radiators, baseboards or in-floor loops. Most homes in the area use forced-air furnaces, so boilers are the exception, and finding a plumber who understands hydronic heat matters.

No heat: what to check

  1. Confirm the thermostat is set to heat and the batteries are fresh.
  2. Check that the boiler has power and the switch is on.
  3. Look at the pressure gauge. It should read in the range on the label, often 12 to 15 psi cold.
  4. Confirm the gas supply and, if equipped, the pilot or igniter.
  5. If the boiler lockout light is on, note any code and call.

Do not relight a pilot if you smell gas. Leave the house, call the gas utility from outside and then call a plumber once it is safe.

Conventional and condensing boilers

Older boilers are non-condensing and vent through a chimney. High-efficiency condensing boilers cool the exhaust enough to form water, so they drain condensate and usually vent through plastic pipe. They can be more efficient, but only if return water temperatures are low enough, which depends on your radiators or in-floor loops.

A plumber considers the pipe size, the radiators, the venting route and the condensate drain before recommending a replacement.

Related systems

Some boilers also supply domestic hot water through an indirect tank. If hot water is the only problem, the issue may be the tank, the zone valve or the aquastat, not the boiler itself. A standalone heater is covered under water heater repair.

Leaks in the heating loop can mimic plumbing leaks. If you suspect hidden wet spots, leak detection can help pinpoint them.

Gas, safety and permits

Gas appliances should be serviced by a qualified professional. Carbon monoxide alarms on every level and near the boiler room are a basic safeguard. The Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, covers plumbing aspects of installations, and local rules may require a permit for replacement. Ask the Village of Carol Stream building staff what applies.

Heating system work may also involve a heating contractor or electrician. The plumber tells you if another trade is needed.

Annual maintenance reduces breakdowns. Ask about a check before the heating season.

Maintenance between visits

  • Keep the boiler room clear of storage.
  • Check the pressure gauge monthly in the heating season.
  • Bleed radiators that are cold at the top.
  • Test carbon monoxide alarms each month.
  • Note any new noises and when they occur.

A written note of dates and symptoms helps the plumber find an intermittent fault faster. If the boiler is also your hot water source, ask how that is plumbed.

What to do if the boiler fails or you smell gas

  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside.
  • Do not use switches, flames or phones near a suspected gas leak.
  • If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outside and call 911.
  • If water is leaking, turn off the boiler and the water feed.
  • Call the 24/7 line once everyone is safe.

Do not try to relight a pilot or reset a lockout repeatedly. A boiler that keeps failing needs a diagnosis.

Boiler Repair and Replacement questions

Do you repair boilers?

Yes, residential hot-water boilers and their pumps, valves and controls.

My radiator is cold at the top. Is that serious?

Usually it is trapped air. Bleeding the radiator often fixes it.

Why does my boiler pressure keep dropping?

A leak or a failed expansion tank is the usual cause.

Should I repair or replace an old boiler?

It depends on age, repair cost and efficiency. The plumber gives both prices.
