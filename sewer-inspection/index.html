Sewer Camera Inspection in Carol Stream, IL

A sewer camera inspection sends a waterproof camera through your drain line. The plumber can show you the pipe instead of guessing.

What the camera reveals

The monitor shows the inside of the pipe in real time. You can see whether the pipe is clay, cast iron or plastic, whether the joints are tight, and how much scale or grease is on the wall. Roots appear as fibrous masses at joints. A belly appears as a stretch where standing water hides the pipe floor.

A locator on the camera head lets the plumber mark the ground above the exact spot, which is how a repair is planned without opening more than needed.

When an inspection is worth booking

  • Before buying a home in Carol Stream or the nearby towns.
  • After a backup that cleared but might return.
  • When a drain is slow at every fixture.
  • Before a basement finish, remodel or landscaping project.
  • When roots, a collapsed pipe or a belly are suspected.
  • After two or more cleanings in a year.

Buyers get the most from it. An inspector may not walk the lateral, and a line that looks fine from the cleanout can be failing 40 feet out.

What happens during the visit

  1. The plumber finds the cleanout or a way into the line.
  2. The camera feeds through the pipe while recording.
  3. Notes are taken on length, material, defects and depth.
  4. A locator marks the location of any defect above ground.
  5. You review the footage together and discuss findings.

If the line is blocked, it is cleared first, because a camera cannot pass a plug. That is why an inspection and sewer line cleaning are often booked together.

What to do before the camera arrives

  • Stop running water if drains are slow.
  • Locate the cleanout, usually near the foundation or in the basement.
  • Keep pets away from the work area.
  • Clear a path to the cleanout.
  • Do not pour chemical cleaners down the drain before the visit.

Chemicals can splash back on the plumber and leave residue that blurs the camera.

Sewer Camera Inspection questions

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Many inspections take under an hour once the plumber has access. Long runs or a blocked line take more time.

Do I need to be home during the inspection?

It helps. You can watch the monitor and ask questions. The plumber can also send you the footage.

Will the camera damage my pipes?

No. The camera head is small, flexible and designed for drain lines.

Can the camera find where a leak is?

It can show a crack or a separated joint. A leak test or tracer may be needed to confirm active leakage.

Is the inspection useful when buying a house?

Yes. It shows the lateral's real condition, which a visual inspection cannot, and it gives you leverage in negotiation.
