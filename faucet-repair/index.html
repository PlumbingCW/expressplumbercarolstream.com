A leak under the sink: the first two minutes

Open the cabinet and look. A wet shutoff valve, a drip from the supply line and a leak at the drain trap look similar but are three different repairs. Turn the oval handle on each valve clockwise until it stops. If the leak slows, the problem was above the valve. If not, it is in the valve or below.

Stuck valves are common because they sit unused for years. Do not force a corroded handle. Turn off the main and call. Same-day service is available when schedules allow, and the dispatcher tells you honestly if it cannot be that day.

Drips, cartridges and washers

A dripping faucet wastes water and wears a ring into the sink. The cause is usually a worn cartridge, a ceramic disc, a washer or an O-ring, depending on the faucet type. Single-handle faucets use cartridges. Older two-handle models use compression washers.

Shutoff valves and supply lines

The small valves under every sink and toilet are the unsung part of a plumbing system. When they stick or drip, the plumber replaces them, ideally with quarter-turn ball valves that are less prone to seizing. Braided supply lines last years but can fail without warning at the crimp.

If a supply line has split, the water can run at full house pressure. That is why an emergency call about a failed line is serious. See burst pipe repair for what to do when a line at the wall is the source.

Sink drains, traps and slow kitchen sinks

A leak at the slip nut under the sink is usually a loose or cracked nut, a worn washer or a misaligned trap. Slow drains often point to a clog in the trap or the branch line. For clogs beyond the trap, see drain cleaning.

Kitchen sinks with a garbage disposal add another leak path at the flange and the dishwasher connection. If the disposal is leaking from the body, garbage disposal repair covers whether a seal or a replacement is the right answer.

Shower and tub valves

A shower that will not shut off, a handle that does not return, and a tub spout that drips all trace to a cartridge, a diverter or a worn valve body. Some repairs require access from the back side of the wall, so the plumber checks for an access panel before opening tile.

Water that lingers in the wall behind a shower valve can stain the ceiling below. If you see a stain, ask about leak detection so the cause is confirmed before repairs.

Faucet and Sink Repair questions

Do you handle faucet and sink emergencies at night or on weekends in Carol Stream?

Yes. Call the 24/7 line and the dispatcher sends a licensed plumber, same day when available.

What if the shutoff valve under the sink will not turn?

Do not force it. Turn off the main water valve and call. The plumber can replace the stuck valve.

Is a dripping faucet really a problem?

A steady drip wastes water and can stain the sink. It usually points to a worn part that is cheap to replace.

Can you install a faucet I bought myself?

Yes. Tell the dispatcher the type and bring the box. Missing parts can delay the job.

Why is the water pressure low at one faucet?

A clogged aerator or cartridge is the common cause. A plumber can clean or replace it.
