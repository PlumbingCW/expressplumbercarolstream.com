A village built fast, and what that means for plumbing

Carol Stream was incorporated in 1959. Its population grew from 836 in 1960 to 31,716 by 1990, according to census counts, and most of its homes went up in those decades. That is long enough for original water heaters, shutoff valves and drain lines to wear out.

The most common failures are predictable. A tank water heater past its twelfth year starts leaking. A shutoff valve that has not turned in years seizes. A drain line with decades of grease and roots slows and then stops.

Water and flooding in the village

The village buys Lake Michigan water through the City of Chicago and says it has no lead service lines in its system. The April 18, 2013 storm put at least four homes under water and closed 25 roads, according to the Daily Herald.

Those facts shape the calls we get. Sump pump failures spike in storms, and hard-water scale is a steady cause of heater trouble. See sump pump repair and water heater repair for the details.
