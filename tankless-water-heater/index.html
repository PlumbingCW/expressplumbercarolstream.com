How a tankless heater works and where it struggles

A tankless heater heats water only when a tap opens. A flow sensor starts the burner or elements, and the unit modulates to hit the set temperature. That removes standby loss and gives endless hot water at one outlet, but the unit has a ceiling on how much it can heat at once. Run two showers and a dishwasher together and the temperature drops.

The most common complaint is a cold-water sandwich: hot water, a brief cold slug, and then hot again. It comes from the unit shutting off during a short pause. Fixing it may mean a flow adjustment, a cleaned filter or a small buffer tank, depending on the model.

Flushing and descaling, step by step

Minerals in the water coat the inside of the heat exchanger. As scale builds, the unit works harder, throws error codes and delivers less heat. Manufacturers usually recommend flushing at least once a year, and more often where the water is hard.

  1. Shut off power and gas, close the water valves.
  2. Connect hoses to the service ports.
  3. Circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for the time the maker specifies.
  4. Flush with clean water and clean the inlet filter.
  5. Reopen valves, restore power and gas, and test for temperature and leaks.

Service ports on each unit are required for the flush to work. A unit installed without them is hard to descale and may need a retrofit.

Non-condensing and condensing models

A non-condensing unit exhausts hot flue gas and needs a stainless steel vent suitable for that temperature. A condensing unit pulls more heat from the exhaust, vents with plastic pipe and needs a drain for condensate. Either way, venting is the part that most often decides the install.

Ask the plumber about venting during the quote. The answer depends on where the unit mounts, how far the vent run is and where the exhaust can safely terminate, and a good quote names the vent material and the termination point.

If the unit is old and parts are scarce, ask for a repair-versus-replace comparison on paper. A newer unit often costs less over the next decade than repeated board and sensor repairs.

Choosing gas, electric or a switch back to a tank

Gas tankless units need a gas line large enough for the burner, usually bigger than a tank heater needs. Electric tankless units need heavy circuits that many panels cannot supply. If either is a problem, a new tank or heat pump installation may be the more practical choice.

Hard water makes tankless service more important. Review the water softener and filtration page for ways to protect the heat exchanger, and use the Village of Carol Stream's own water quality report as the starting point for what is in the supply.

What to do if the unit shows a fault or you smell gas

  • If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility from outside.
  • Turn off the unit's power and close the gas valve if it is safe.
  • Note the error code on the display.
  • Close the cold-water valve if the unit is leaking.
  • Call the 24/7 line once everyone is safe.

Do not open the cabinet of a gas unit yourself. The plumber handles the burner and the vent.

Tankless Water Heater questions

How often should a tankless water heater be flushed?

Many manufacturers call for an annual flush. Hard water may call for more often, so check the manual for your model.

Why does my tankless heater give hot, cold, then hot water?

The unit shuts off during a short pause in flow and restarts. Cleaning the filter, adjusting settings or adding a buffer can reduce it.

Can a tankless heater be added to a house that had a tank?

Often yes, but gas line size, venting and a drain for condensate decide the cost.

Do tankless units need a drain?

Condensing models do for the acidic condensate. All models benefit from a floor drain nearby for flushing.

Do you service tankless heaters at night in an emergency?

Yes, the line is open 24/7. If the unit leaks or smells of gas, shut off the supply and call right away.
