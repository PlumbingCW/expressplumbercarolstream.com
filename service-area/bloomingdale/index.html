Old Town and the newer subdivisions

Bloomingdale was incorporated in 1889 and re-incorporated on June 18, 1923. The 2020 census counted 22,382 residents and 9,301 housing units. Old Town, at Bloomingdale Road and Lake Street, has some of the oldest buildings, while the neighborhoods around Stratford Square Mall came much later.

Older buildings may have cast iron drains and galvanized pipe. Newer ones have plastic drains and copper or PEX supply. The plumber figures out which you have on arrival.

What a water heater call looks like

  1. Tell the dispatcher whether the tank is leaking, cold or noisy.
  2. Turn off the gas or power to the heater and close the cold-water valve on top.
  3. Place a bucket under any leak and photograph the label.
  4. The plumber confirms the cause and quotes repair or replacement.
  5. You approve the price before work starts.

A leak from the tank body means replacement. A leak from a valve or fitting may be a quick repair. See water heater repair and tankless water heater for options.

Hidden leaks in finished spaces

Finished basements and second-floor bathrooms hide leaks behind drywall. A water bill that jumps with no change in habits, a musty smell or a ceiling stain are the early signs.

Acoustic and thermal methods let a plumber locate a leak without opening the whole wall. Leak detection explains how that works. Ask before anyone cuts drywall.

Finished basements and old leaks

Many Bloomingdale homes have finished basements with drywall and carpet over pipes that were never opened up again. A leak in that setting grows slowly and quietly, so catch the early signs. A damp smell, a stain at the baseboard or a rising bill all deserve a call.

Tell the dispatcher about any finished space near the problem. The plumber then plans the access so walls are opened only where needed.
