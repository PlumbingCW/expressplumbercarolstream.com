Where the village sits

The Village of Glendale Heights was incorporated on July 13, 1959, and the 2020 census counted 33,176 residents in 11,874 housing units. Its main roads include Glen Ellyn Road, North Avenue, Fullerton Avenue and Bloomingdale Road, and Public Works is at 1615 Glen Ellyn Road.

Most of the housing went up in the decades after incorporation, so a lot of homes are now old enough for original water heaters, tired supply valves and older drain lines to fail.

Flooding in April 2013

On April 18, 2013, heavy rain closed about 20 roads in Glendale Heights and flooded eight homes, according to the Daily Herald, while Carol Stream saw 25 road closures that day. Anyone who lived here then remembers the sump pumps running nonstop.

Storms like that test every pump. If yours is more than ten years old, or if it ran hard during a past storm, have it checked. The page on sump pump repair lists the checks you can make yourself, and basement waterproofing covers longer-term fixes.

Water supply and what it means for fixtures

Glendale Heights buys Lake Michigan water from the DuPage Water Commission. The commission describes its water at about 8 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard, so white scale on faucets and in kettles is normal here.

Scale is slow, not an emergency, but it shortens water heater life. Draining a tank once a year helps, and a plumber can show you how. For a tank that is failing, see water heater replacement.

Common calls in a 1960s-onward village

  • Water heater leaking from the base.
  • Toilet that keeps running or will not flush.
  • Kitchen sink that drains slowly.
  • Basement floor drain bubbling.
  • Sump pump running without stopping.
  • No hot water on a cold morning.

Each of these has a quick check that helps the dispatcher decide how fast to send someone. Describe the symptom plainly and say when it started.

Planned work in Glendale Heights

Many calls here are scheduled, such as water heater swaps, new sump pumps with battery backup and basement bathroom plumbing. The village has about 11,874 housing units, and a good share are approaching the age where major fixtures wear out.

Storm season checklist

  • Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and watch the pump start.
  • Clear window wells and downspouts.
  • Know where the main water valve is.
  • Charge or replace the backup battery.
  • Keep the 24/7 number saved in your phone.

A few minutes before storm season costs nothing and can save a basement.
