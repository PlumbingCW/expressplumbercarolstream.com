Two counties, one village

Hanover Park was incorporated in 1958 and sits in both Cook and DuPage counties. The 2020 census counted 37,470 residents. Main roads include U.S. 20 (Lake Street), County Farm Road and Ontarioville Road, and the Ontarioville neighborhood has its own history.

The Metra Hanover Park station on the Milwaukee District West Line brings commuters through town, so many households keep irregular hours and need service outside a nine-to-five day.

Lake Michigan water through a joint agency

The village gets all of its water from Lake Michigan through the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency, which in turn buys from Chicago. The village's 2020 lead sampling showed a 90th percentile of 0 ppb.

Those are good results, but your own plumbing still matters. Old solder or fixtures inside a house can differ from the village main. Ask for a plumber's opinion if you plan to renovate.

Toilets, tubs and everyday fixtures

  • A tub that drains slowly.

Most of these are same-day repairs. Toilet repair and faucet and sink repair describe the usual causes.

When a drain keeps coming back

A drain that clogs every few weeks is not a clog. It is a pipe problem, like roots, a belly or grease buildup. Cleaning it each time only treats the symptom.

A camera shows what the pipe looks like, and drain cleaning with the right method can clear the line properly. If the pipe is damaged, repair is better than endless cleanings.

Planned work in Hanover Park

Households in Hanover Park book fixture swaps, water heater replacement and drain cleaning on a schedule as well as in emergencies. Since the village sits in both Cook and DuPage counties, rules for permits can differ by address, so ask the village building office.

For a worn tank, see water heater replacement. For a persistent clog, see sewer line cleaning.

Owners, renters and who to call first

Whether you own or rent changes the first call. A tenant usually starts with the landlord, who may have a plumber on contract. A homeowner can call the 24/7 line directly.

If you rent, shut off the fixture valve, take photos and contact your landlord, then call us if the landlord authorizes it or if the emergency cannot wait.

Preparing for a visit

  • Clear the area around the fixture or appliance.
  • Put pets in another room.
  • Turn off the water to the fixture if you can.
  • Have the brand and model of the appliance ready.
  • Plan for the water to be off for a short time.

A little preparation shortens the visit and lowers the chance of a second trip.
