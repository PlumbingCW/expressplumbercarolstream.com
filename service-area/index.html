If your town is not listed

Call the line anyway. Nearby towns such as Itasca and Warrenville are close to Bloomingdale and Wheaton, and the dispatcher can often tell you quickly whether a visit is possible.

Give the dispatcher your street address and ZIP code. They confirm availability and explain what to do while you wait. Never delay a call about active flooding because your town is not on the list.

Why local knowledge helps

A plumber who knows the area knows the housing eras, the common materials and the water. A 1960s ranch in one town and a 1990s colonial in another do not fail in the same ways.

Our base in Carol Stream, ZIP 60188, puts us within reach of the towns above. That is why the line can usually offer same-day appointments when available.

Planned and emergency work in each town

Emergencies are the reason people find us, but most towns also see planned work such as water heater swaps, remodels, softener installs and sewer inspections before a home sale. The same line books both.

If you are planning a project, call during the day and describe what you want. The dispatcher arranges a visit to scope it and explains how the price will be set.

Roads and landmarks we know

Crews working out of Carol Stream know the main routes between the towns. North Avenue runs through Carol Stream, Glendale Heights and Bloomingdale. Army Trail Road, Gary Avenue and St. Charles Road cross the village.

Local landmarks such as Armstrong Park, the Great Western Trail and the Town Center help the dispatcher confirm your location by phone when you are not sure of an address.

What to have ready when you call

  • Your street address, ZIP code and the nearest cross street.
  • A description of what you see, hear or smell.
  • The location of the main water shutoff, if known.
  • Whether anyone in the home is at risk.
  • A number where the plumber can reach you.

Having these ready shortens the call and lets the dispatcher send the right plumber with the right equipment.
